## §1 Introduction & System Context

### §1.1 Motivation and role of Domain 1 *(criterion: role of Domain 1, spec-driven approach)*

Public transport capacity planning and simulation depend on aligning three data families that are rarely born in the same place: planned supply (schedules), observed demand (passenger counts), and external demand drivers (holidays, events, weather). The value of systematically archiving and joining the first two has been demonstrated for transit performance management: Furth, Hemily, Muller and Strathman (2006) show that archived AVL/APC data supports schedule debugging, running-time analysis and service planning once it is validated and made queryable at trip level. Multi-agent transport simulation reinforces the point at the methodological level: MATSim-style calibration requires a transit schedule *and* empirical demand observations as first-class inputs (Horni, Nagel & Axhausen, 2016). Within the Smart Mobility Würzburg project, Domain 1 (WVV data engineering & infrastructure) therefore assumes the role of the foundational data platform and interface owner for Domain 2 (SUMO/MATSim traffic simulation), Domain 3 (capacity planning & ML) and the shared visualization dashboard. We frame this ownership deliberately along Dehghani's data-mesh thinking — each domain publishes and consumes well-defined, domain-owned data products rather than ad-hoc exports (Dehghani, 2019) — which turns the cross-domain boundary in §5 from a courtesy into an architectural principle. *(sources: S5.4, S2.20c, S1.5)*

### §1.2 Core requirements *(functional + non-functional)*

The platform's functional requirements follow directly from its role: a unified stop-event history, calendar-resolved daily schedules, joinable context features (school terms, university calendars, events, weather), and extensibility toward continuous real-time feeds. The non-functional requirements codify how the platform earns trust: *reproducibility* from immutable raw files, *provenance* of every derived row, *append-only* history without destructive updates, and *resilience to schema drift*. These map onto established principles: the FAIR guiding principles demand that data be findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable — with persistence in the raw layer as a precondition for reuse (Wilkinson et al., 2016); Batini and Scannapieco's quality framework (2016) supplies the dimensions (completeness, accuracy, timeliness, consistency) against which incoming WVV feeds must be profiled rather than blindly ingested; and the event-sourcing pattern formalizes append-only event capture as the auditable basis for reconstructing any past state (Azure Architecture Center, 2026). Schema evolution, finally, is treated as a governed process — compatibility classes (backward/forward/full) as practiced in schema registries show that drift can be managed contractually instead of silently (Confluent, 2026). The consequence for architecture and pipeline design is the paper's running theme: immutable raw copies are the ground truth, and every correction is additive and attributable. *(sources: S1.1, S1.3, S1.4, S4.30)*

## §2 Mobility Data Sources & Exchange Standards

### §2.1 Static schedule standards: GTFS vs. NeTEx/Transmodel *(criterion: evaluate data sources and formats)*

Two static schedule standards dominate the European landscape. GTFS is a flat, CSV-based feed specification — agency, routes, trips, stop_times plus the calendar/calendar_dates service-exception mechanism — maintained by MobilityData as the de-facto interchange format of routing and simulation tools (MobilityData, 2026); its ubiquity extends to our downstream consumers, since Eclipse SUMO ships a GTFS importer (DLR, 2026) and MATSim ecosystems rely on GTFS-to-schedule converters (matsim-org, 2026). NeTEx, by contrast, is the XML serialization of Transmodel (published as the EN 12896:2022 series), offering richly structured, versioned frames with explicit DayTypes and service patterns (CEN, 2022; Transmodel-CEN, 2024). Coverage is not the differentiator: the EU-maintained GTFS ↔ NeTEx mapping demonstrates that all GTFS and GTFS-RT information can be expressed in NeTEx (DATA4PT, 2024), and EU Delegated Regulation 2017/1926 binds National Access Points to NeTEx-related European standards (European Union, 2017). What *does* differentiate is representation and auditable processability: flat, deterministic GTFS-style rows can be validated row-by-row — which is what extracting 2026 PDF timetables demands — whereas deeply nested NeTEx graphs add validation complexity without adding information our platform needs. We therefore adopt GTFS as the internal conceptual anchor: upstream, the relevant German archive (DELFI) publishes GTFS; downstream, SUMO and MATSim consume GTFS natively; and the internal data model can be validated with simple row-level contracts. *(sources: S2.1, S2.19, S2.20, S2.6, S2.8, S2.7, S2.13)*

### §2.2 Real-time and operational APIs: GTFS-RT vs. VDV/SIRI/TRIAS *(criterion: evaluate data sources and formats)*

For operational data, the same standard-setting organizations split along the same line. GTFS-Realtime is a lightweight Protobuf protocol with three message types — TripUpdates, VehiclePositions, ServiceAlerts — whose identifiers deliberately reference the static GTFS feed (MobilityData, 2026), making it cheap to consume but presupposing that someone re-publishes German operator data in it. German operators instead exchange operational data through VDV-Schrift 453, the Ist-Daten-Schnittstelle, with VDV 454 layered on top for connection management (VDV, 2018), while travel-information request/response services are standardized as TRIAS (VDV 431-2), a SIRI-derived German profile (VDV, 2019); SIRI itself is the European EN 15531 service interface for real-time public transport information (CEN; SIRI-CEN, 2026). Regionally, such data is distributed through hub infrastructure like DEFAS, Bavaria's central mobility-data platform connecting operators and authorities (Mobilitätsplattform Bayern, 2026), and nominally surfaced via Germany's National Access Point, the Mobilithek (Mobilithek, 2026). The practical consequence is that there is no single unified live stream for a regional operator such as WVV: formats (453/SIRI vs. TRIAS vs. GTFS-RT), hub access rules and coverage vary. Our design therefore treats real-time ingestion as a pluggable adapter concern — raw payloads land in the object store unchanged, and normalization happens later (§4.6) — rather than coupling the lake to any single protocol. *(sources: S2.3, S2.10, S2.11, S2.12, S2.9, S2.18, S2.14)*

### §2.3 Assessment of the concrete WVV project sources *(criterion: evaluate data sources and formats)*

Applying this lens to the WVV data landscape yields three partitions. *Soll (planned supply):* weekly Germany-wide DELFI GTFS snapshots (2022–2025), obtained via the OpenData ÖPNV portal's DELFI dataset (OpenData ÖPNV, 2026; cf. gtfs.de, 2026), empirically matched 100% of WVV's 2023 "Baufahrplan" (construction timetable) including SEV replacement services — strong evidence that the archive covers the network — but regenerate `route_id`/`trip_id` on every export, violating the ID-stability intent of the GTFS best practices (MobilityData, 2026), a deficiency the community acknowledges openly in the specification repository (google/transit, PR #171). The 2026 schedule gap is covered by ~65 per-line WVV PDF timetables. *Ist (observed demand):* 5.1M APC/AVL stop-event rows (2022–2026, 40 lines) in Parquet, exhibiting the classic syndromes the literature warns about — empty placeholder files and schema drift (35 columns pre-2026 vs. 42 in 2026), i.e. incomplete and inconsistent delivery (Batini & Scannapieco, 2016). *Context:* interval data for Bavarian public/school holidays, university lectures, and events, plus hourly pedestrian street counters, and — as a planned external ML feature — historical weather from the Deutscher Wetterdienst's Climate Data Center (DWD, 2026). This partitioning motivates the treatment in §3–§5: versioned schedule snapshots with entity resolution, schema-tolerant ingestion, and calendar-resolved context joins. *(sources: S2.15, S2.16, S2.2, S2.5, S1.3, S5.10)*

## §3 Storage & Spatial Database Architectures

### §3.1 Workload analysis *(criterion: compare spatial database architectures)*

Before comparing products, the workload itself must be classified, because spatial capability and analytical throughput are different axes. Domain 2's simulation stack performs *topological GIS workloads* — routing, map-matching, polygon operations on the OSM road graph — inside SUMO/MATSim and its preprocessing tools; Domain 1 does not replicate these. Domain 1's workload is an *analytical data platform workload*: multi-million-row time-series scans (5.1M stop events and growing), historical snapshot versioning and diffing, temporal joins of Ist × Soll × calendar at daily grain, and bulk feature exports for ML and simulation calibration, with spatial attributes amounting to fixed stop coordinates. The database literature is unambiguous about which storage layout serves such read-mostly analytical scans: column-oriented systems read only the accessed columns, compress aggressively, and vectorize execution (Stonebraker et al., 2005; Abadi, Boncz, Harizopoulos, Idreos & Madden, 2013; Boncz, Zukowski & Nes, 2005). This classification is the criterion for the paradigm comparison in §3.2 and, later, for the section of the cross-domain contract (§5) that forbids D2 workload migration into D1. *(sources: S3.2, S3.4, S3.3)*

### §3.2 Comparative evaluation of storage paradigms *(criterion: compare spatial database architectures)*

Three paradigm families are credible candidates. (1) *Relational spatial RDBMS:* PostgreSQL with PostGIS offers OGC-conformant geometry types, spatial GiST indexing and strict ACID semantics (PostGIS, 2026; PostgreSQL, 2026) — the strongest choice for interactive geospecific queries, editing and network maintenance. Its row-oriented storage, however, pays per-row overhead on wide analytical scans and on historical multi-snapshot deduplication, workloads its engine was never shaped around (Stonebraker et al., 2005; Abadi et al., 2013). (2) *NoSQL stores:* document databases ingest nested, schema-variable API payloads comfortably (MongoDB, 2026), and wide-column/key-value systems such as Cassandra, Bigtable and Dynamo deliver exceptional write throughput and partition tolerance by restricting the consistency and query model (Lakshman & Malik, 2010; Chang et al., 2006; DeCandia et al., 2007); the survey literature is equally clear that ad-hoc relational joins and analytical aggregations are precisely their weak axis (Davoudian, Chen & Liu, 2018) — but `Ist × Soll × Calendar` joins are Domain 1's bread and butter. (3) *Lakehouse architecture:* open, columnar file formats on cheap object storage, decoupled from the compute engine that reads them (Armbrust, Ghodsi, Xin & Zaharia, 2021), with Parquet as the interchange representation (Apache Parquet, 2026) and the S3 API as the ubiquitous, provider-neutral interface (AWS, 2026) — implemented here by the open-source RustFS object store (RustFS, 2026). *(sources: S3.7, S3.8, S3.2, S3.4, S3.10, S3.11, S3.12, S3.13, S3.14, S3.1, S3.9, S3.15, S3.16)*

### §3.3 Architectural synthesis: why S3 + ClickHouse for WVV *(decision)*

Matching the workload analysis against these families yields the decision: an S3-compatible object store as the immutable raw zone, coupled with ClickHouse as the columnar analytical engine. ClickHouse's MergeTree family provides append-only, LSM-style table parts with asynchronous merges and full columnar compression — designed exactly for the scan and aggregate patterns of §3.1 — and its publication record documents sparse primary indexes, wide support for arrays (a natural fit for per-trip stop-time vectors) and exceptional throughput on time-series queries (Schulze et al., 2024; ClickHouse, 2026). The `s3()` table function queries Parquet in the object store directly, so raw and conformed layers share one storage boundary, while ReplacingMergeTree/`argMax` semantics give append-only versioning without destructive updates (ClickHouse, 2026). The lakehouse argument — that open formats under decoupled storage/compute preserve reproducibility and tool freedom — is explicitly adopted (Armbrust et al., 2021). The boundary case is named honestly rather than hidden: if Domain 1 ever absorbed OSM road-graph processing for Domain 2, PostGIS with its GiST spatial index would become the preferable home for that sub-workload (PostGIS, 2026); as specified, that work stays in Domain 2's SUMO/MATSim toolchain. *(sources: S3.5, S3.6, S3.9, S3.1, S3.7)*

## §4 ETL/ELT Pipeline & Continuous Processing

### §4.1 Processing paradigm and orchestration *(criterion: design robust ETL pipelines for continuous processing)*

The first pipeline decision is processing style. The Lambda architecture formalizes immutable input data plus a batch layer and a speed layer serving merged views (Marz & Warren, 2015); Kreps's Kappa critique observes that maintaining two code paths for the same logic is the architecture's core cost, avoidable when one stream processor suffices (Kreps, 2014), with Kafka-style log infrastructure as its substrate (Kreps, Narkhede & Rao, 2011). At WVV's volumes — roughly 1.1M APC rows per year, weekly GTFS archives, and daily simulation-calibration cycles — the latency that streaming buys is negligible while its operational complexity is real; continuously scheduled micro-batches polling external APIs deliver "continuous processing" in the required sense. Orchestration then becomes the reliability mechanism: against plain cron, we select Apache Airflow for its DAG-based dependency tracking, automatic retries, data-interval semantics and systematic backfill — the features that make multi-year snapshot reprocessings reproducible (Apache Airflow, 2026). Dagster's asset-centric model and Prefect's lighter flow abstraction were evaluated as credible alternatives (Dagster, 2026; Prefect, 2026), but Airflow's maturity for backfill-and-retry batch regimes matches the fingerprint/snapshot pattern of §4.4, where historical re-execution must be first-class. *(sources: S4.5, S4.6, S4.8, S4.10, S4.11, S4.12)*

### §4.2 Medallion layering over an immutable raw zone *(criterion: design robust ETL pipelines for continuous processing)*

Layering follows the medallion convention: bronze preserves raw fidelity plus provenance, silver houses type-harmonized and schema-unified but append-only observations, and gold exposes the deduplicated business truth (Databricks, 2026). Our implementation shifts layers into ClickHouse schemas over an S3 object store that remains the single source of truth — a lakehouse positioning (Armbrust et al., 2021) that directly satisfies the FAIR reusability requirement that raw data persists untouched (Wilkinson et al., 2016). Keeping *all* silver observations is a deliberate dimensional-modeling inheritance: slowly-changing-dimension thinking shows that retaining superseded rows with validity attribution is how a warehouse supports both "what do we believe now" and "what did we believe then" (Kimball & Ross, 2013). Practitioner criticism that medallion layering multiplies copies and compute is acknowledged (cf. §6.1) and mitigated here by columnar compression and by keeping the *physical* raw copy singular — layers are materialized tables, not re-ingested files. *(sources: S4.3, S3.1, S1.1, S4.1)*

### §4.3 Single-point calendar resolution *(design pattern)*

GTFS encodes service calendars as recurring date ranges plus per-date exceptions (`calendar`, `calendar_dates`), with a service day that may extend past midnight — times above 24:00 are explicitly part of the specification (MobilityData, 2026). Re-resolving these rules in every downstream query would scatter identical logic across ML features, simulation exports and dashboard views, a classic non-conformed-dimension smell in warehouse design (Kimball & Ross, 2013). The pipeline therefore expands `calendar` + `calendar_dates` exactly once, per operating day in `Europe/Berlin`, into concrete calendar-date rows that simultaneously absorb Bavarian holiday intervals, university lecture periods and event intervals. Downstream consumers join on a plain date key and never re-implement calendar semantics; school-day and public-holiday cases relevant to WVV footnoted trips arrive pre-resolved. This is where the context CSVs of §2.3 enter the platform as first-class dimensions — the daily grain required by Domain 3's utilization models is created exactly here. *(sources: S2.1, S4.1)*

### §4.4 Cross-snapshot entity resolution: content fingerprinting *(design pattern)*

Because DELFI exports regenerate `route_id` and `trip_id` on every snapshot (§2.3), identifiers cannot join history — the feed effectively arrives with its keys broken relative to best-practice expectations (MobilityData, 2026), and the GTFS specification community discusses precisely this stability gap (google/transit, PR #171). Entity resolution theory frames our problem as matching records across snapshots on quasi-identifiers and then consolidating, with the consolidation step determining truth (Christophides, Efthymiou & Stefanidis, 2020). We use a deterministic quasi-identifier: a content hash (fingerprint) of the invariant trip attributes — line, direction/terminus, stop-ID sequence, arrival/departure minutes — which is reproducible, auditable and cheap to validate; probabilistic record-linkage models in the tradition of Fellegi & Sunter (1969) are rejected because they introduce coupling and unverifiable match probabilities where an exact functional key suffices. Our fingerprint design deliberately stores *all* observations in silver while gold resolves effective truth via `argMax(..., snapshot_date)` (ClickHouse, 2026), exposing `was_corrected` flags and `snapshot_count` confidence — quality signals in the sense of Batini & Scannapieco (2016). The approach’s sensitivity to minute-level retimings is admitted and treated in §6.1; its premise — that stable identity must be *constructed* above unstable GTFS exports — is being recognized in current research on recurring-schedule identification [verify authors] (2026). *(sources: S2.2, S2.5, S4.15, S4.16, S3.6, S1.3, S4.17)*

### §4.5 2026 PDF extraction with a deterministic validation contract *(design pattern)*

The 2026 schedules exist only as ~65 heterogeneous WVV PDF layouts; preliminary coordinate/rule-based grid parsing failed across them — an expected outcome, since even dedicated deep-learning table-detection research treats layout heterogeneity as the hard problem [verify authors] (arXiv 2211.08469). Generative LLMs, by contrast, perform zero-shot structured extraction across document types (Wei et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2024), and table-centric work indicates LLMs can process layouts well enough to attempt at document scale (Lu et al., 2025). Their documented hallucination behavior [verify authors] (arXiv 2311.05232; arXiv 2404.18930) means extraction can never be trusted raw; it is therefore wrapped in a deterministic validation contract: extracted trips must validate against the known DHID stop list, monotonic stop sequences, GTFS-style time formats, and explicit per-trip school-day footnotes — expressed as schema-validated outputs (JSON Schema, 2026) in the spirit of declarative expectation suites (Great Expectations, 2026). Trips that fail the contract drop into a manual review queue; accepted rows enter the identical Medallion flow as a `source='pdf'` pseudo-snapshot. The pattern — trust the model for perception, trust the contract for truth — is the paper's answer to unstructured input without sacrificing reproducibility. *(sources: S4.22, S4.23, S4.19, S4.20, S4.26, S4.27, S4.29)*

### §4.6 Continuous real-time extension *(design pattern)*

Live data completes the pipeline without changing its shape: an adapter polls operational feeds (GTFS-RT where available; TRIAS/SIRI via DEFAS for Bavarian context, cf. §2.2) and lands raw payloads unchanged in the S3 raw zone — the same ingestion contract as files, preserving replayability (Armbrust et al., 2021). GTFS-RT entities reference static GTFS identifiers (MobilityData, 2026), but because German live feeds use operator-side trip keys (VDV, 2018) and can carry re-timed or cancelled trips, volatile live identifiers cannot simply be stored: they are joined to Gold *fingerprints* via per-snapshot lookup tables derived from §4.4, making live observations as joinable as schedule observations. Research on correcting published timetables from observed real-time feeds [verify authors] (rt2gtfs, 2026) shows the analytical payoff of such alignments — empirical travel-time distributions flowing back into schedule truth — and frames the Phase-2 outlook (§6.3) of replacing *Soll* fingerprints with observed corrections rather than treating the two as separate worlds. *(sources: S4.18, S3.1, S2.3, S2.10, S3.6)*

## §5 Cross-Domain Data & API Contract

### §5.1 Ubiquitous join keys *(criterion: define the API contract)*

Every contract needs keys all parties can rely on. Spatial identity uses the German stop register DHID (`de:09663:*` for Würzburg), whose format and semantics are defined in VDV-Schrift 432 (VDV, 2016) and distributed in the central DELFI stop register (DELFI, 2025), descending structurally from IFOPT (EN 28701); it bridges GTFS `stop_id`s, LLM-extracted PDF stops, APC station codes and simulation nodes, with area/quay levels available when resolution is needed. Network identity uses stable GTFS `route_short_name` (deliberately including `SEV` replacement services, since the 2023 construction period proved their relevance) plus a normalized direction/terminus. Temporal identity is the ISO `service_date` in `Europe/Berlin`, aligned to the GTFS operating-day semantics including times above 24:00 (MobilityData, 2026). Trip identity is the content fingerprint of §4.4 — the only key that survives export instability. These four keys are the platform's public ABI: they are declared once here and referenced by every payload and view in §5.2, in line with the data-product expectation that interfaces, not codebases, are what domains share (Dehghani, 2019). *(sources: S2.17, S2.1, S1.5)*

### §5.2 What Domain 1 provides — outputs and access patterns *(criterion: deliverables)*

The deliverables follow the consumers. To **Domain 2**, D1 exposes versioned stop coordinates (`gold_stop`), active line metadata with `route_type`, and daily trip schedules with arrival/departure minute arrays — the shape SUMO's `gtfs2pt` and MATSim converters ingest natively (DLR, 2026; matsim-org, 2026) — plus empirical APC boarding/dwell distributions for calibration, exactly the archived-APC use case Furth et al. (2006) document. To **Domain 3**, D1 provides the schema-harmonized `clean.stop_events` (2022–2026), Soll-Matched trip views for delay and utilization modeling, and pre-joined calendar/event/lecture/weather feature tables (weather anchored in the DWD archive, 2026) at the daily grain of §4.3. To the **dashboard**, D1 publishes fast analytical SQL views for KPIs (line utilization, punctuality, schedule coverage) and sink tables for simulation scenarios and ML forecasts. Access is twofold and deliberately boring: read-only SQL/HTTP query access for interactivity (ClickHouse, 2026), and reproducible Parquet/CSV exports in S3 for ML training and simulation runs, preserving the open-format reproducibility the lakehouse argument promises (Apache Parquet, 2026; Armbrust et al., 2021). *(sources: S2.19, S2.20, S5.4, S5.10, S5.2, S3.9, S3.1)*

### §5.3 What Domain 1 needs — inputs and open questions *(criterion: needs)*

The interface is bidirectional, and the input side is specified as requests. From **Domain 2**: whether `shapes.txt` geometries or custom OSM stop-to-link mappings must be persisted in D1 (this decides §3.3's PostGIS boundary question), and the output schemas of simulation runs for the sink tables of §5.2. From **Domain 3**: the target temporal/spatial aggregation grain (the daily exploded grain of §4.3 is tunable), additional external feature requirements — historical weather from the DWD Climate Data Center being the established candidate (DWD, 2026), given that weather and calendar effects on ridership are well documented empirically [verify authors] (weather–ridership studies: Pierce County bus study; extreme-weather study; calendar-events study) — and the schema for forecast/optimized-schedule write-back, which must reuse the fingerprint key to be composable. From **WVV stakeholders**: documented semantics for the undocumented APC fields (`quality_factor`, `passenger_*_measured` vs. derived counts, raw Parquet timezone) — indispensable because the APC literature shows counter data is only usable after validation and quality filtering (Kimpel et al. [verify year]; Furth et al., 2006) — plus access details for live operational feeds via DEFAS (§2.2). Each request is a contract item: unambiguous keys, documented schemas, defined owners. *(sources: S5.10, S5.11, S5.12, S5.6, S5.4, S2.18)*

## §6 Critical Discussion & Conclusion

### §6.1 Key architectural trade-offs

Three trade-offs deserve explicit defense. (1) *Daily exploded schedule grain vs. compact date ranges:* exploding services to concrete calendar days duplicates rows, but at columnar compression ratios and scan patterns this cost is small and buys zero-cost temporal joins at exactly the grain ML and simulation consume (Kimball & Ross, 2013; Abadi et al., 2013; Schulze et al., 2024). (2) *Content fingerprints:* they solve ID instability across snapshots, yet a minute-level retiming legitimately changes identity, splitting one real-world trip into two fingerprints. The mitigation is deliberate: truth resolution and correction flags operate at line/time-window level through `argMax`-based revision metadata (`was_corrected`, `snapshot_count`), so minor retimings surface as corrections rather than as identity claims — a pragmatic answer to the stable-identification problem current research is formalizing [verify authors] (2026). (3) *Medallion layering:* multiplying materialized layers costs storage and recompute (practitioner critiques are blunt about this), but here the raw copy is singular, layers are schemas in one columnar engine, and every layer boundary carries provenance and validation value (Databricks, 2026; Wilkinson et al., 2016). Net: trade-offs are accepted where they purchase joinability, auditability, or reproducibility, and engineered around where they don't. *(sources: S4.1, S3.4, S3.5, S3.6, S4.17, S4.3, S1.1)*

### §6.2 Limitations and data risks

The platform's honest risk register has four entries. (1) *APC sensor semantics:* `quality_factor` and `passenger_*_measured` flags are undocumented vendor fields; the APC literature demonstrates that count accuracy hinges on exactly such validation parameters, with error rates at the percent level even in validated European bus fleets (Kimpel et al. [verify year]; Furth et al., 2006; European APC studies [verify authors], 2019, 2025) — until WVV documents them, Gold aggregates over measured fields must be treated as best-effort. (2) *Export completeness:* 2026 APC Parquet arrives with placeholder-empty files, so ingestion must surface "gap vs. zero" distinctions (Batini & Scannapieco, 2016). (3) *PDF ambiguity:* per-trip school-day footnotes fall through to the manual-review queue of §4.5 whenever the validation contract cannot confirm the intended reading — hallucination research argues this human gate stays mandatory [verify authors] (arXiv 2311.05232). (4) *Live coverage:* whether WVV data is actually exposed via DEFAS or a national feed remains unconfirmed; the Regulation's NAP mandate (European Union, 2017) is a legal frame, not a delivery guarantee. Quality assurance in this platform is therefore not a final inspection step but distributed: contracts at ingestion (§4.5), provenance flags in resolution (§4.4), and explicit residual risks here. *(sources: S5.6, S5.4, S5.8, S5.9, S1.3, S4.26, S2.13)*

### §6.3 Conclusion and Phase-2 outlook

The paper's thesis holds together in one sentence: heterogeneous municipal mobility data can be unified without GIS-transactional overhead by combining an immutable S3 raw zone, an append-only medallion discipline, and a columnar OLAP engine — with content fingerprinting and contract-validated LLM extraction patching exactly the points where real-world feeds break their own standards. Each ingredient is inherited rather than invented: immutable raw storage operationalizes FAIR reuse (Wilkinson et al., 2016), append-only resolution follows event-sourced and SCD practice (Azure Architecture Center, 2026; Kimball & Ross, 2013), the lakehouse decoupling is argued at research level (Armbrust et al., 2021), and the domain boundaries follow data-product thinking (Dehghani, 2019). For Phase 2, two extensions are natural and the architecture anticipates both: real-time correction loops that let observed Ist data re-qualify Soll truth (rt2gtfs [verify authors], 2026), and ML feature expansion along the already-conformed calendar/weather joins. The open questions of §5.3 are the implementation team's first backlog, not design debt. *(sources: S1.1, S1.4, S4.1, S3.1, S1.5, S4.18 — plus the input-side list in §5.3)*
