Introduction and System Context

Public transport has seen increased interest in Germany, being perceived as part of a larger solution for the country's economic stagnation (MCube / TU Munich, 2025), its failure to meet statutory climate targets (Umweltbundesamt, 2025), and socio-economic inequality in access to mobility (Salomo, 2025). Effective public transport requires not just technological advancements but also effective planning. Supply needs to be matched with demand. Trains, trams, and buses need to drive along routes that people want to use, arrive at sensible times, and be reliable. Unreliability shows up in running-time variance and recovery cost, and archived data is how you find it (Furth, Hemily, Muller & Strathman, 2006). The Smart Mobility Würzburg project approaches this planning problem from two sides, through traffic simulation (Domain 2) and capacity planning (Domain 3). A simulation, statistical analysis, or prediction can only be as good as the underlying information. Therefore, the following term paper will cover Domain 1: the methodology and data engineering to serve as a foundation. It will give an overview of the provided sources (WVV), argue how adding structured third-party data can help with the shortcomings of the existing corpus, compare storage architectures, outline the required transformations, design data contracts, and give an outlook into a potential future live-data integration.
